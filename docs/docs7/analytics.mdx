Every Docs7 site collects two kinds of analytics at the edge, with nothing to install and no cookie banner: AI crawler traffic and human page views. Both live in the site's dashboard.

## AI traffic

AI systems are becoming a primary reader of documentation, and Docs7 shows you that traffic directly. Every request from a known AI crawler is recorded with its company, bot name, path, and country. The dashboard shows:

- Totals for the selected window: requests, verified requests, unique bots, unique paths.
- A daily chart broken down by company.
- The paths AI agents fetch most, searchable, so you can see whether they read `llms.txt`, the raw `.md` exports, or specific guides.
- A live tail of individual requests as they happen.

Recognized crawlers currently include Anthropic (`ClaudeBot`, `Claude-User`, `Claude-SearchBot`) and OpenAI (`GPTBot`, `ChatGPT-User`, `OAI-SearchBot`).

### The verified flag

A user agent string is trivial to fake. Docs7 checks every AI request's IP address against the ranges the vendor publishes (`claude.com/crawling/bots.json`, `openai.com/gptbot.json`, and so on) and marks the request as verified when it matches. The dashboard reports both counts, so scraper traffic wearing a ClaudeBot user agent is visible instead of inflating your numbers.

## Human analytics

Document navigations from real browsers are counted separately, with AI crawlers and generic bots filtered out. You get visitors and page views over time, top pages, referrers, and countries.

Visitors are counted with a hash of the day, site, IP address, and browser that rotates daily. No raw IP address is ever stored, there are no cookies, and no script is injected into your pages; counting happens entirely at the edge.

## Scope and windows

- Analytics cover all of a site's traffic: the production hostname, branch previews, and custom domains. Each teamspace sees only its own sites.
- The time window is 7 days by default and goes up to 90.
- New requests appear in the dashboard live, within a few seconds.
